# Test speech — General Public Speaking

A speech written deliberately against the General Public Speaking rubric, for
checking that a genuinely good speech scores well. Roughly 260 words, about
1 minute 45 at a controlled pace.

Delivery cues are in brackets. Do not read them aloud — they tell you what to
do with your voice.

---

Ladies and gentlemen. [pause]

Three years ago I sat in a hospital corridor at two in the morning, watching my
father try to say my name. [pause] He had had a stroke on the Tuesday. By
Thursday, the man who taught me to argue could not find the word for water.

[breathe]

I want to talk to you tonight about recovery, and about a number our health
system has quietly got wrong.

[firmly] Here is that number. Six weeks.

That is how long intensive rehabilitation is funded for after a stroke. [pause]
But in 2019, The Lancet followed stroke patients for two years, and found
meaningful recovery continuing at eighteen months. [emphasise] Eighteen months,
not six weeks.

[slower] We stop paying in week six. The brain does not stop healing in week
six.

[breathe]

I have spent the three years since that corridor reading everything I could
find on this, and speaking to the physiotherapists who do the work. They all
say the same thing. The improvement is still there. The funding is not.

[softly] My father got his words back. Not because the system worked, but
because my mother drove him to therapy twice a week for a year after the money
ran out. [pause] Most families cannot do that.

[with conviction] So recovery stops being medicine. It becomes a question of
who can afford it.

[louder] So here is what I ask of you.

The next time your representative votes on rehabilitation funding, ask them one
question. [pause] [slower] Ask them what the evidence says about month seven.

[warmly] Thank you.

---

## Why this should score well

| Criterion | Weight | How it is addressed |
|---|---:|---|
| Structure & shape | 20 | Story opening → thesis → three-part body → callback close on "month seven" |
| Logos | 20 | The Lancet 2019, eighteen months vs six weeks, a specific funding gap |
| Ethos | 15 | First-hand experience, plus three years of reading and speaking to clinicians |
| Pathos | 13 | The corridor, the word for water, the mother driving him twice a week |
| Language & clarity | 10 | Short sentences, concrete nouns, deliberate repetition of "six weeks" |
| Pace, pausing, fluency | 22 | Cues space the delivery; no filler words in the text |

## How to use it

Read it aloud in the Speaking Coach with **General Public Speaking** selected.
Ignore the bracketed cues as words — act on them instead. Then run **Deeper
Analysis** to see whether the delivery score tracks the content score.

If the coach scores this in the 70s or 80s, the calibration is working. If it
lands in the 40s, the scoring is still too harsh and worth another look.
